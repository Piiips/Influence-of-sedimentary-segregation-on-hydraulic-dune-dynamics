# Annotated Bibliography — Internal Structure & Segregation of Dunes and Bedforms

Focus: internal dynamics, grain sorting, and depositional architecture of dunes/bedforms in sand beds, spanning laboratory experiments, aeolian (wind) systems, and hydraulic (subaqueous) systems. Compiled to support the JFM manuscript on the internally-segregated structure of bidisperse hydraulic dunes.

Papers are grouped by theme. Each entry gives the citation, topic, key findings, and a link. Open-access items are marked **[OA — downloadable PDF]**; the rest are behind publisher paywalls (link goes to the abstract/record).

---

## 1. Direct observation of internal segregation in migrating dunes (experimental)

### Groh, Rehberg & Kruelle (2011) — *Observation of Density Segregation inside Migrating Dunes*
**Phys. Rev. E 84, 050301(R).** **[OA — arXiv:1109.3885]**

*Topic.* Downscaled model dunes in a narrow water flume (6 mm wide, flow 0.6 m/s, Re ≈ 36000) built from equally-sized beads of two densities (glass 2.5 g/cm³, ceramic 6.0 g/cm³) to isolate *density* segregation from size effects.

*Key findings.* Counterintuitively, the heavier grains accumulate in the central core near the **top** of the migrating dune, while lighter grains are buried at the base. The result is robust to the initial packing (heavy-on-top, heavy-on-bottom, mixed all converge to the same attractor) and to volume fraction. Three mechanisms: (i) lighter grains carried on longer flights, (ii) light grains trapped in the lee recirculation forming an elongated "nose," (iii) light grains form a carpet the dune rolls over. Migration velocity scales as v ∝ ρ_mean^(−3/2) at constant volume, extending Bagnold's inverse density–velocity relation. Directly relevant analogue for internal architecture of a bidisperse hydraulic dune.

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.3885

## 2. Bidisperse subaqueous barchans (experimental)

### Cúñez, Franklin et al. (2021) — *Growth of barchan dunes of bidispersed granular mixtures*
**Physics of Fluids / arXiv:2104.02178.** **[OA — arXiv]**

*Topic.* Systematic water-channel experiments tracking two-species barchan dunes with varied grain size, density and concentration under controlled flow.

*Key findings.* Overall barchan morphology stays roughly constant regardless of the mixture, but the two species redistribute internally and on the surface: denser and smaller grains segregate onto/within the barchan while the complementary species is preferentially exported. Provides quantitative growth laws for bidisperse barchans and a clean experimental link between grain properties and where each species ends up inside the bedform.

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02178

## 3. Grain sorting on the lee side / grain flows (subaqueous)

### Kleinhans (2004) — *Sorting in grain flows at the lee side of dunes*
**Earth-Science Reviews 65, 75–102.** (paywall; local copy present: `Sorting in grain flows at the lee side of dunes.pdf`)

*Topic.* Review + analysis of how sediment mixtures are sorted as grain flows (avalanches) redistribute material down the lee slope of subaqueous dunes.

*Key findings.* The internal grain-size pattern of cross-strata is set by three controls: (1) pre-sorted sediment delivered to the brink (governed by superimposed bedforms and flow unsteadiness), (2) differential deposition + grain flows on the lee slope, and (3) reworking within the lee-side separation zone. Establishes the conceptual framework this project's model builds on.

Link: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0037073804000660

### Rousseaux, Caps & Wesfreid (2004) — *Granular size segregation in underwater sand ripples*
**Eur. Phys. J. E 13, 213–219.** **[OA — arXiv:cond-mat/0403682]**

*Topic.* Laboratory study of size segregation in ripples formed under oscillatory water flow.

*Key findings.* Demonstrates size-selective sorting within subaqueous ripples and connects the sub-surface segregation pattern to the near-bed hydrodynamics, giving a small-scale hydraulic analogue of the sorting seen in larger dunes.

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0403682

## 4. Vertical sorting in bedforms (hydraulic, flume)

### Blom, Ribberink & de Vriend (2003) — *Vertical sorting in bed forms: flume experiments with a natural and a trimodal sediment mixture*
**Water Resources Research 39(2), 1025.** (paywall)

*Topic.* Flume experiments quantifying grain-size-selective transport and the vertical sorting profile built into migrating dunes.

*Key findings.* Documents pronounced upward-fining within subaqueous dunes and a feedback between vertical sorting and dune irregularity; the sorted internal structure controls the composition of transported sediment as a function of trough-scour depth. Foundational data for sorting-evolution models.

Link: https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2001WR001088

### Blom, Ribberink & Parker (2008) — *Vertical sorting and the morphodynamics of bed-form-dominated rivers: a sorting evolution model*
**J. Geophys. Res. Earth Surface 113, F01019.** (paywall)

*Topic.* Continuum model coupling bedform migration to the evolution of the vertical sorting profile.

*Key findings.* Reproduces observed upward-fining and the interaction between the active (bedform) layer and a substrate; provides a modelling template for predicting internal stratification from mixture transport — directly comparable to the analytical approach of this project.

Link: https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2006JF000618

## 5. Lee-slope grainfall & avalanche processes (aeolian)

### Anderson (1988) — *The pattern of grainfall deposition in the lee of aeolian dunes*
**Sedimentology 35, 175–188.** (paywall)

*Topic.* Physically-based model of grainfall (settling of saltating grains) on the dune lee, and how it primes avalanching.

*Key findings.* The grainfall length scale is smaller than the lee-slope length, so the slope oversteepens and fails; a predictable "pivot point" separates erosional source from depositional accumulation regions of grainflow deposits. Initial failure coincides with the steepest gradient in grainfall, just downslope of the grainfall maximum. Supplies the initial geometry for grainflow cross-strata.

Link: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1365-3091.1988.tb00943.x

### Makse (2000) — *Grain segregation mechanism in aeolian sand ripples*
**Eur. Phys. J. E 1, 127–135.** (paywall; already in `citas.bib`)

*Topic.* Mechanistic model of segregation in aeolian ripples.

*Key findings.* Two competing mechanisms — size segregation from different hopping lengths in the dune/ripple wake, and *shape* segregation during rolling along the surface — produce "inverse-grading climbing-ripple lamination" and cross-stratification, respectively.

Link: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s101890050014

## 6. Cross-stratification: bedform superimposition & unsteadiness (experimental)

### Reesink & Bridge (2007, 2009) — *Influence of bedform superimposition and flow unsteadiness on the formation of cross strata in dunes and unit bars (Parts 1 & 2)*
**Sedimentary Geology 202 (2007) & 222 (2009).** (paywall)

*Topic.* Flume experiments isolating the controls on grain-size sorting and geometry of cross-strata.

*Key findings.* Bedform superimposition (ripples/bedload sheets on dunes; ripples/dunes on unit bars) plays a far larger role in generating cross-stratification than traditionally recognized; unsteady flow modulates the pre-sorting delivered to the brink and hence the internal architecture.

Links: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0037073807000747 · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0037073809002176

## 7. Continuum granular segregation theory (applicable to lee-slope flows)

### Trewhela, Ancey & Gray (2021) — *An experimental scaling law for particle-size segregation in dense granular flows*
**J. Fluid Mech. 916, A55.** (paywall; local copy present)

*Topic.* Experiments deriving a scaling law for the segregation velocity of a large/small intruder in dense granular shear flows.

*Key findings.* Gives a closed-form, shear-rate- and size-ratio-dependent segregation flux — the constitutive ingredient needed to model kinetic-sieving sorting on a dune lee slope. Central reference for the theoretical closure in this project.

Link: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/experimental-scaling-law-for-particlesize-segregation-in-dense-granular-flows/

### Gray & collaborators (2020) — *Bridging the gap between particle-scale forces and continuum modelling of size segregation: application to bedload transport*
**arXiv:2005.09435 (J. Fluid Mech.).** **[OA — arXiv]**

*Topic.* Links grain-scale forces to continuum segregation theory, applied to bedload.

*Key findings.* Provides a physically-grounded segregation-flux closure connecting DEM-measured forces to continuum advection–diffusion segregation equations — the theoretical bridge between granular physics and sediment-transport-scale bedform models.

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09435

## 8. Non-invasive imaging of internal dune architecture (aeolian, field)

### Bristow et al. — *Internal structure of aeolian dunes determined using ground-penetrating radar*
(Abu Dhabi study; also *Aeolian Sediments* Wiley chapter.) (paywall / academia.edu copies)

*Topic.* GPR imaging of cross-stratification and bounding surfaces inside active dunes without excavation.

*Key findings.* Wind-parallel profiles reveal dipping cross-strata sets and 2nd/3rd-order bounding surfaces; wind-transverse profiles show trough cross-stratification from oblique barchanoid progradation. Main dune sets 5–8 m thick with foresets up to ~23 m; reversing winds nest complex sets. Field validation of the depositional architecture that segregation models aim to predict.

Link: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781444303971.ch5

## 9. Foundational reference

### Bagnold (1941) — *The Physics of Blown Sand and Desert Dunes*
**Methuen (Dover reprint 9780486439310).** (book)

*Topic.* The founding text on aeolian transport, ripples/dunes, grain size-grading, and internal structure of accumulated sand.

*Key findings.* Introduces the wind–sand transport framework, size-grading of grains, and the inverse relation between dune migration velocity and grain density that later experimental work (e.g. Groh et al. 2011) extends to bidisperse systems.
